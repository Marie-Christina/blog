Kyoto Day Hike: Nanzen-ji to Ginkaku-ji via Mt. Daimonji

Kyoto Temple-to-Mountain Walking Guide

travel japan kyoto hiking temples

At a Glance

Route: Nanzen-ji to Ginkaku-ji over Mt. Daimonji
Distance: 3.79 miles
Elevation Gain: 1,339 ft
Difficulty: Moderate with steep sections and rocky steps

Overview

Kyoto is packed with major temples, but this route gives you a quieter way to connect two of the best known sites on foot. The hike starts at Nanzen-ji, climbs through the Higashiyama mountains to Mt. Daimonji, then descends to Ginkaku-ji.

It is a one-way day hike with panoramic city views, forested trail sections, and several hidden stops along the way, including a small waterfall shrine.

Getting to the Starting Point

The easiest access is by Kyoto subway. Take the Tozai line to Keage Station and use exit 1. Walk downhill, pass through the Keage pedestrian tunnel, and continue to the Nanzen-ji entrance.

Bring water and snacks before you start. There are limited options near the trailhead and no vending machines once you are on the mountain path. Wear proper walking shoes, sun protection, and clothing suitable for temple visits.

Nanzen-ji Temple Complex

Nanzen-ji is one of Kyoto's most important Zen temple sites. You can pass through the Sanmon gate, explore the main grounds, and optionally visit side buildings and gardens before heading uphill.

At the back of the complex, you will find the brick aqueduct, still in use and one of the most photogenic landmarks in this area.

Trailhead and the Higashiyama Climb

The trail begins near the back parking area of Nanzen-ji. Look for the path on the right side and start climbing. Early on, there is a small waterfall and shrine often called Oku-no-in, a peaceful hidden stop before the forest sections.

Most of the path is narrow and quiet, with cedar trees and occasional ruins. If conditions are wet, skip optional side loops after heavy rain because footing can become difficult.

Mt. Daimonji Viewpoint

At the top clearing, you get one of the best panoramic views of Kyoto. This is a great place to pause and identify city landmarks like Kyoto Tower.

Mt. Daimonji is also a spiritual site. During August Obon season, fires are lit in the shape of the kanji for fire as part of the Okuribi tradition.

Descent to Ginkaku-ji

The descent is straightforward and busier than the climb. You can typically reach Ginkaku-ji in about 30 minutes from the upper trail area. Keep left around the small building at the clearing and continue down.

Once you reach the temple district, you can visit Ginkaku-ji and explore nearby snack shops, cafes, and small souvenir spots.

Returning to Central Kyoto

Bus is the easiest way back. Ginkakuji Mae serves line 32, while nearby Ginkakujimichi has additional options including lines 5, 203, and 204.

There is no nearby subway station by Ginkaku-ji, so bus or taxi will usually be the most convenient return after the hike.
